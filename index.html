Kaushik Mohan

SCSS Wednesday June 10 · 9:05 AM – 9:25 AM

The Language of Sequence Analysis

State Sequence Analysis (SSA), used in the social sciences for longitudinal and life-course research, is limited by simpler probabilistic models and non-generative non-parametric models. Our broader research explores the use of generative Transformer architectures, the structural foundation of Large Language Models (LLMs), to address the unique constraints of categorical sequence analysis. Current work evaluates the efficacy of training small Transformer models for sequence imputation and representation learning.

Muskan Walia

Wednesday June 10 · 9:25 AM – 9:45 AM SCSS

Stress-testing Generative AI in High-Stakes Policy Settings: Evidence from Race-Blind Charging in California

Generative AI systems are rapidly moving from prototypes to embedded components of government decision-making infrastructure. For instance, California mandates all prosecutors implement a new review procedure called “race-blind charging”, where prosecutors review case documents with race-related information redacted. To make this feasible, the state encouraged prosecutors to use AI-based redaction. We designed and tested one such system that uses generative AI to automatically redact race-related information from police reports. We present these validation results, demonstrating that our algorithm reliably removes all race-related indicators required by law, reduces the ability to predict an arrestee’s race from redacted narratives, and performs at the top of its class relative to existing alternatives.

Jawed Nazari

Int'l Ed

Talk Title: Member-Level Integration of Refugee Families in the United States: A Qualitative Multiple-case Study of Afghan Families Resettled After 2021

This study examines how refugee family integration unfolds unevenly within the same household. Focusing on Afghan families who arrived in the United States after 2021, it asks how spouses and children experience and negotiate life differently as they encounter schools, workplaces, service agencies, and legal systems. The study is guided by four mechanisms: structural conditions and institutional encounters, gender-order renegotiation, intergenerational timing gaps and youth brokering, and family systems and identity/belonging. Using a qualitative multiple-case study of Afghan families in New York and New Jersey, the study offers a member-level framework showing how integration is produced through everyday household negotiation.

George Perrett

SCSS

Talk Title: Flaws in the LLM Automation Narrative

Large Language Models are frequently advertised as operating at the level of human experts, and the prospect of automating knowledge economy work is offered as justification for the high cost of developing and deploying them. Using a novel benchmark design that explicitly measures response variance and error magnitude, we show that the output of a frontier Large Language Model on a computer coding task is highly variable and frequently contains extreme errors. These findings undermine the automation narrative and demonstrate the importance of considering variance and error magnitude in benchmark evaluations.
